Reading 4:"Opinion | Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It" Article Reflection

After reading W.J. Hennigan’s “Opinion | Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It” (2024), this entire article is entirely fake due to the lack of evidence or any form of facts that can prove the Nuclear War is occurring. In the terms with Digital Media, this article derives from the concept of “keeping up” and “unlimited scrolling.” the intention of W.J. Hennigan wants us to be persuaded by the article, by writing it in such a way to promote panic and fear. The part of “keeping up” relies on how he wants us to catch up on what is happening in the present time, to alert us on the issue, but is also causing people to feel unsafe. The unlimited amount of “keeping up” turns to “endless scrolling,” causing us to believe in anything even when it is not true. This article reminded me of what Rebecca Coleman, a famous theorist explained in her article, “Making, managing and experiencing ‘the now’: Digital media and the compression and pacing of ‘real-time,’” (2020). She even explains how the internet, especially stories such a s this one, persuade you to immediately scroll through the page where it creates confusion and lack of focus because of overdone imagery built with AI. She interviews one of her participants, John, on how mindless scrolling can affect one’s attention span. Coleman explains, “The mindless scrolling explained here is a kind of elongated now in that it stretches out ‘the now’. The present is ‘fall[en] into’ and the lack of focus that John describes spreads the moment out. The now of scrolling, then, refers not only to how the present may be extended in terms of how long it lasts but also because of its affective and experiential intensity,” (pp. 1693). The “experiential intensity” of the “keeping up and scrolling” come from how Hennigan’s article uses a lot of AI generated images in creating the illustration of the Nuclear War, but it causes the viewer to lose attention. Besides, there is not enough information for the Nuclear War to occur, because the government controls the internet where it puts various pieces of mixed information that make us feel like it is all a lie.

In the previous paragraph I discussed mindless scrolling and AI, another way this article does not appear to be believed is the overuse of AI images. AI Slop has caused nothing but chaos in the modern world, where people are becoming vulnerable to be persuaded by the propaganda of the government or online writers to scare them. Hennigan’s story is also using dramatic imagery in creating the illusion of fear, but it can be debunked to other resources in proving their lies. The endless scrolling of the images caused me to lose focus on what exactly I was reading on his security opinions on the Nuclear War that is occurring. The AI images minimizes the possible significant impact of the story, where it just loses the viewer's attention. Overall, the article makes a point on the situation with the three countries, but it would be better without the use of overdramatic images, resulting in the viewer having difficulty in reading the article.
