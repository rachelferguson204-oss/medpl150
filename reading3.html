Reading 3: Motherhood in Crisis, the Tragedy of Heela

After reading all the stories of Motherhood In Crisis Stories From Sierra Leone by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager, and Saidu Bah, it struck me in the heart painfully. I never understand how many countries around the world, even in America have still to this day expressed neglect and indifference towards the discrimination against women. These stores focuses on the themes of sexual assault, injustice, and lost of innocence in explaining the trauma each character in each chapter faces. Although all of them saddened me in a way, chapter two “Heela: The schoolgirl ‘My stolen future,’” was the most traumatizing chapter in the graphical novel. From reading the chapter, I felt unease in how the perpetrator/rapist got away living free without accountability for what he did to Heela. When reading the graphic novel, part of my mind told me why she didn’t tell anyone after it happened or fought against him during the part, but it also made me understand why it was done this way. The reason behind her assault comes from the fact how she wasn’t protected nor taught how to protect herself because of how societies in other countries, especially in poor countries have little to no force of safety due to the corruption in their government, just like in the U.S.

In understanding Heela perspective, its the fact of how intimidated and scared she was when she was raped by the the guy. The rapist did what he did as a way to show a sense of power and fear over her because he saw her as an object of pleasure and a tool to dehumanize. In reality, he is nothing but a coward who was never disciplined nor punished. This issue is because of how the men in the country of Sierra Leone are shown to practice heinous acts against their own people, especially the women, to resemble a form of dominance. However, the gut who raped is Heela took her innocence and hope, leaving her in despair with being pregnant with his child. The title of the chapter “‘My Stolen Future’” derives from how Heela’s wishes and potential future was destroyed due to her trauma of her innocence and happiness was taken away from her. The chapter focuses on themes of sexual assault, injustice, and intimidation due to how unprotected and vulnerable Heela was in this part of her life. Despite the aftermath of her now taking care of her child, whose father is his mother’s rapist, it really sickens me to see how trapped and tortured Heela is to be forced to live in such pain. This pain is something that she is forced to carry for the rest of her life as a burden, making her feel like less than a human.

In the previous paragraphs, I discussed on how horrible it feel to be hurt by the abuse and not get the justice one deserves, but my main issue is how neglectful and uncaring her father was in not having the heart to check up on his daughter from time to time, even if she was living with her grandmother. I felt that the father was the biggest problem because of how terrible of a father he was to Heela, but would show care for the other children. No matter what happens in life a real father or any real parent is supposed to take initiative and have it in their heart to protect their children and teach how to navigate in the world safely. If the father was more caring and understanding to his daughter, none of what happened to her would have happened if he taught her to beware of her surroundings, know to avoid danger, and tell someone if someone is pressuring her or forcing her to do something against her will. In the end, the story was very touching and made me feel empathic to Heela and the treatment she received from the assault, learning to forgive herself and acknowledge her life with being a proud mother to child, teaching him the right things in life.
